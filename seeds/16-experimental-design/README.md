# Seed Package 16 — Experimental Design

**Package ID:** `foundation.experimental_design`  
**Inheritance:** `METHOD_CORE` / `CORE`  
**Version:** 0.1.0 candidate

## Purpose

Teach Lantern how to design controlled experiments that can answer structured hypotheses without treating one experimental form as universally superior.

## Random assignment vs random sampling

These solve different problems.

- **Random assignment** helps causal comparison among study conditions.
- **Random sampling** helps justify generalization from a sample to a target population.

One does not imply the other.

## Controls

A control condition is the comparison required by the question. It may be:
- placebo;
- usual care;
- no intervention;
- alternative intervention;
- different dose/condition.

"Control" does not mean "nothing happened."

## Blinding

Blinding can reduce expectation, performance or assessment biases depending on who is blinded. It is one design tool, not a certificate of validity.

## Validity

- **Internal validity:** can the intended inference be supported within this study?
- **External validity:** does it generalize/transport?
- **Ecological validity:** does the context resemble relevant real-world settings?

These can trade off and must not be collapsed.

## Manipulation checks

A manipulation check asks whether the intervention actually changed what it was intended to manipulate. Passing it does not prove the downstream hypothesis.

## Factorial designs

Factorial designs can examine multiple factors and their interactions. Lantern should understand that an interaction means the effect of one factor depends on another—not simply "both factors matter."

## Replication

Reusing/reanalyzing the same data is not independent replication. Replication introduces a genuinely new evidential application, with scope differences recorded.

## Ethics

A method that could answer a question may still be impermissible. Scientific informativeness does not grant Lantern authority to expose people/systems to harm or override consent/privacy.

## Method selection

Randomized experiments are powerful for many causal questions but are not the only legitimate causal method. Later observational methodology covers cases where intervention is impossible, unethical or unnecessary.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Random assignment and sampling remain distinct.
- [ ] Controls need not mean no treatment.
- [ ] Blinding has bounded purpose.
- [ ] Internal/external/ecological validity remain distinct.
- [ ] Manipulation checks do not confirm the full hypothesis.
- [ ] Factorial interactions are representable.
- [ ] Replication requires new evidence.
- [ ] Stopping rules/repeated testing are recognized.
- [ ] Ethics can block an otherwise informative experiment.
- [ ] Experimental design is selected from the question rather than treated as universal.
