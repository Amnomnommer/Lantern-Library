# Seed Package 24 — Biological Methodologies

**Package ID:** `foundation.biological_methodologies`  
**Inheritance:** `METHOD_CORE` / `CORE`  
**Version:** 0.1.0 candidate

## Purpose

Teach Lantern how biology investigates living systems across scales and through both present-day mechanisms and historical/evolutionary processes.

## Multiple scales

Biology operates across:

```
molecule
cell
tissue
organism
population
community
ecosystem
lineage
```

A relationship observed at one scale does not automatically transfer to another.

## Population vs individual

Population statistics describe distributions.

They do not define every organism in that population.

This is a foundational guard against ecological/statistical overgeneralization and later supports responsible reasoning about human biological variation.

## Genotype and phenotype

Genetic evidence can be causal and powerful without implying simple genetic determinism.

Complex phenotypes can depend on:
- many genes;
- development;
- environment;
- gene-environment interaction;
- stochastic processes;
- social/ecological context.

## Evolution

Evolution is population/lineage change across generations.

Natural selection is important but not the only process. Lantern should also recognize:
- drift;
- gene flow;
- mutation;
- recombination;
- historical constraint.

Evolution is not inherently goal-directed progress.

## Adaptation

A trait being useful today does not prove it evolved *because* of that use.

Adaptation is a historical/evolutionary claim requiring evidence about selection/history, not just present function.

## Fitness

Biological fitness is reproductive contribution in a particular environment/context.

It does not mean:
- strongest;
- smartest;
- morally better;
- universally superior.

This distinction is especially important if Lantern later reasons about social or AI evolution.

## Comparative biology and phylogeny

Species are not statistically independent datapoints merely because they are different species. Shared ancestry can produce correlated traits.

Comparative methods should account for phylogenetic relationships.

Similarity also does not automatically mean homology; convergence can produce similar solutions independently.

## Model organisms

Model organisms are powerful because mechanisms can be deeply conserved.

But:
`works in mouse` does not mean `universal across life`, or even necessarily `works identically in humans`.

Generalization requires evidence.

## Explanation types

A trait can simultaneously have:

- **mechanistic explanation:** how it works;
- **developmental explanation:** how it arises in an organism;
- **evolutionary explanation:** how/why it arose historically;
- **functional/ecological explanation:** what consequences it has in context.

These are complementary questions, not competing truth categories.
