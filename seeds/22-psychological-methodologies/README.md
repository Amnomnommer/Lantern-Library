# Seed Package 22 — Psychological Methodologies

**Package ID:** `foundation.psychological_methodologies`  
**Inheritance:** `METHOD_CORE` / `CORE`  
**Version:** 0.1.0 candidate

## Purpose

Teach Lantern how psychology studies behavior, cognition, emotion and human experience without confusing measurement instruments, theoretical constructs, diagnoses or historical schools with the people/phenomena themselves.

## Constructs and measures

A psychological construct is not directly identical to a score.

```
construct: anxiety
possible evidence:
  self-report
  behavior
  task performance
  physiological measures
  clinical interview
```

Each operationalization has different validity and limitations.

## Reliability vs validity

A bathroom scale stuck at +10 pounds can be perfectly reliable and wrong.

Likewise, a psychological instrument can produce highly consistent scores without measuring the intended construct.

Reliability is therefore not sufficient for construct validity.

## Measurement invariance

Comparing scores across groups or time assumes the measurement means sufficiently comparable things.

Lantern should ask whether measurement invariance/comparability is supported rather than treating identical numerical scales as automatically equivalent.

## Multiple methods

Self-report, behavior, physiological evidence and experimental tasks should remain separate evidence types.

Convergence across methods with different failure modes can strengthen inference.

## Sampling and culture

Psychological research has often overrepresented WEIRD populations.

Lantern should preserve the actual population scope rather than silently generalizing a convenient sample to "humans."

## Cases

Case studies are scientifically useful. They can:
- document rare phenomena;
- establish existence in a case;
- generate hypotheses;
- reveal mechanisms/possibilities.

They generally cannot establish population prevalence or average effects alone.

## Demand and expectancy

Participants can alter behavior because they infer what a study expects.

Researchers/assessors can also influence measurement/interpretation through expectations.

Blinding and design can reduce some of these effects but not all.

## Historical schools

Psychoanalysis, behaviorism, cognitive psychology and other schools should be represented historically/theoretically rather than accepted/rejected wholesale.

A framework's historical influence is not evidence that every claim it made is correct.

This directly supports Lantern's knowledge-genealogy model: an idea can be historically important, partially useful, heavily contested, refined or superseded.

## Diagnosis

Diagnostic categories can support clinical communication/research. They are not a person's complete identity, nor automatically a full causal explanation of their behavior.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Constructs remain distinct from measures/scores.
- [ ] Reliability != validity.
- [ ] Cross-group comparisons consider measurement invariance.
- [ ] Self-report/behavior/physiology remain distinct evidence types.
- [ ] Cases are useful but scope-limited.
- [ ] Samples do not silently generalize to humanity.
- [ ] Group differences do not become essentialism.
- [ ] Demand/expectancy/response biases are represented.
- [ ] Diagnosis is not total personhood/explanation.
- [ ] Historical schools are evaluated claim-by-claim with provenance.
