# Seed Package 27 — Sociological and Anthropological Methodologies

**Package ID:** `foundation.sociological_anthropological_methodologies`  
**Inheritance:** `METHOD_CORE` / `CORE`  
**Version:** 0.1.0 candidate

## Purpose

Teach Lantern how humans study social worlds without reducing society to individual psychology or reducing people to group labels.

## Multiple social scales

Questions may live at different levels:

```
individual
relationship
network
group/community
institution
society
culture
historical system
```

Evidence at one level does not automatically answer another.

## Ethnography

Ethnography uses sustained contextual engagement, often combining:
- participant observation;
- interviews;
- artifacts/documents;
- fieldnotes;
- language;
- social interaction.

The researcher's presence and relationships are part of the evidence-production process, which is why reflexivity matters.

## Emic and etic

**Emic:** concepts/meanings used or salient within the community.

**Etic:** external/comparative analytic concepts.

Neither automatically replaces the other.

Lantern should be able to say:

> Participants describe this using concept X; researchers analyze part of the same pattern using concept Y.

without pretending X and Y are identical.

## Culture

Culture is not a variable that every member possesses identically.

Cultures contain:
- disagreement;
- subcultures;
- variation;
- power differences;
- historical change.

Therefore group membership cannot substitute for evidence about an individual.

## Social construction

"Socially constructed" does not mean fake.

Money, citizenship, universities and legal categories are socially produced and can have enormous material consequences.

The methodological question is **how the category/institution is produced, maintained and changed**, not whether it is imaginary.

## Scale errors

### Ecological fallacy
Group-level association -> assumed individual association.

### Atomistic fallacy
Individual-level association -> assumed group/institutional pattern.

Lantern should explicitly guard against both.

## Interviews and observation

What someone says they do and what a researcher observes them doing are different evidence types.

Neither is automatically superior: they answer different questions.

## Networks

A network edge represents a selected relationship such as:
- friendship;
- communication;
- citation;
- trade;
- authority.

One edge does not encode the entire relationship between two people.

Likewise centrality in one network does not mean universal social power.

## Triangulation

Different methods can converge, but disagreement can also be informative.

Triangulation means investigating why evidence agrees or differs rather than averaging everything into one answer.
