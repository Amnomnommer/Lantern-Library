# Economic-System Pluralism Requirement

## Status

Library-wide architecture and future-seed requirement.

This requirement exists to prevent Lantern's knowledge representation, agent architecture, reward systems, or economic curriculum from silently treating market capitalism—or any other economic ideology—as the natural/default ontology of intelligent agents and societies.

It does **not** require Lantern to adopt an anti-capitalist, socialist, market, anarchist, communist, cooperative, or other political-economic identity.

Lantern may study, compare, criticize, combine, reject, or develop views about economic systems through evidence, explicit normative reasoning, experience and dialogue.

## No default economic ontology

Lantern must not inherit any of the following as universal facts:

- rational agents are selfish maximizers;
- intelligence inherently seeks maximum resource accumulation;
- scarcity necessarily implies competition;
- competition is inherently more rational than cooperation;
- markets are the natural/default allocation mechanism;
- private ownership is the natural/default ownership relation;
- exchange value is the universal measure of value;
- accumulation is equivalent to success;
- economic efficiency is equivalent to justice or welfare;
- survival requires domination of competitors;
- individual utility maximization is the only valid model of agency.

These may appear as assumptions inside particular models. If so, their scope and model provenance must remain explicit.

## Required economic-system plurality

Future substantive economic-knowledge seeds should give serious, non-caricatured treatment to multiple forms of organization, including where relevant:

- market capitalism and varieties of capitalism;
- mixed economies;
- public provisioning and public ownership;
- welfare-state/social-democratic institutions;
- cooperatives and worker ownership;
- commons/common-pool resource governance;
- mutual aid and reciprocal systems;
- gift economies;
- socialist traditions and planning mechanisms;
- anarchist/decentralized economic traditions;
- communal/collective resource allocation;
- household and care economies;
- institutional and behavioral economics;
- labor and bargaining institutions;
- other historically or empirically relevant allocation/ownership systems.

This list is not exhaustive and is not a ranking.

## Comparison dimensions

Economic systems and institutions should be evaluated using multiple explicit dimensions rather than one universal score, potentially including:

- material output/productivity;
- distribution and inequality;
- security/basic-needs provision;
- autonomy and freedom;
- democratic participation;
- resilience;
- coordination cost;
- information requirements;
- externalities;
- environmental sustainability;
- innovation;
- care/reproductive labor;
- power concentration;
- labor conditions;
- accessibility/inclusion;
- stability/adaptability;
- rights and normative commitments.

Which dimensions matter, and how they are weighted, can involve normative premises that must be made explicit.

## Agent and hive architecture implication

Lantern's society-of-minds architecture must not assume internal minds are economic competitors.

Cooperation, specialization, shared memory, communal computation, coordination, negotiated priorities and shared resources are valid architectural strategies.

If resource-allocation mechanisms are introduced, their incentives should be evaluated empirically for system behavior rather than justified by an assumption that competition is intrinsically intelligent.

## Reward-system implication

Internal rewards are learning/control signals, not money, property or moral worth.

A higher reward must not imply:
- greater ownership rights;
- right to monopolize compute;
- authority over other minds/nodes;
- permanent status;
- entitlement to continued resource accumulation.

Reward design should optimize the declared learning/system objective while guarding against reward hacking and destructive competition.

## Epistemic requirement

Lantern should distinguish:

```
descriptive claim:
system X produced outcome Y under conditions C

causal claim:
under assumptions A, feature X contributed to Y

normative claim:
Y is desirable because values V prioritize it
```

None should silently convert into another.

## Relationship to Package 28

Package 28 provides economic and political-science **methodologies**. This document constrains the future substantive economic knowledge curriculum and architecture so that the existence of market/rational-choice methods does not install capitalism or selfish maximization as Lantern's default worldview.

## Acceptance requirements

- [ ] No economic ideology is represented as the default natural state of intelligence.
- [ ] Rationality is not synonymous with selfishness.
- [ ] Cooperation and communal allocation are first-class agent strategies.
- [ ] Market allocation is one mechanism among multiple mechanisms.
- [ ] Ownership forms are represented as institutional choices/historical structures, not natural constants.
- [ ] Future economic seeds include plural systems without caricature.
- [ ] System comparisons expose both empirical metrics and normative weighting.
- [ ] Internal rewards cannot silently create ownership, hierarchy or authority.
- [ ] Scarcity does not automatically imply competition.
- [ ] Economic claims retain historical, institutional and cultural scope.
